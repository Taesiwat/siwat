Everyday Economics: The Supermarket Miracle

If you walk into a supermarket in any major city, you are standing in the middle of a beautiful, complex system. On the shelves, there are fresh apples from New Zealand, rich chocolate from Belgium, and fragrant rice from Thailand.

These items did not arrive there by accident. No government leader ordered a cargo ship to cross the ocean just to fill your dinner plate. Instead, this miracle happens because millions of people, who do not even know each other, are making choices under constraints.

Many people think economics is only about stock markets, interest rates, and dry government budgets. But at its heart, economics is deeply human. It is the science of how we make choices when things are limited. Because while our time, money, and resources are limited, our wants and needs are infinite.

To help us understand this chaotic world, Harvard economist N. Gregory Mankiw created the "Ten Principles of Economics". If we look closely at these rules, we can understand why governments succeed or fail, why markets are so powerful, and why we make the choices we do every single day.
